# 7E. Matched betting: the one way to "print money" that isn't a prediction

**Not backtested.** It is arithmetic on bonus terms, and bonus terms vary by
book, state and month. Treat every number here as an estimate to check
against the actual offer before acting.

## How it works

Sportsbooks give new customers bonus bets ("bet $5, get $200 in bonus
bets"). A bonus bet pays only the winnings, not the stake. You place the
bonus bet on one side at long odds and hedge the other side (a second book or
an exchange) so that **you get roughly the same cash whichever side wins**.

For a bonus bet of size B at decimal odds o, hedged on the other side at odds
h, the hedge stake is H = B·(o − 1)/h. Locked profit ≈ B·(o − 1) − H. At long
odds (o ≈ 4–6) this usually converts **65–75% of the bonus into cash**.

## Worked example

| | |
|---|---|
| Bonus | $200 in bonus bets (after a $5 qualifying bet) |
| Bonus bet | $200 at +400 (decimal 5.0) on Team A at Book 1 |
| Hedge | Team B at decimal 1.25 at Book 2: stake = 200·4/1.25 = **$640** |
| If A wins | +$800 at Book 1, −$640 at Book 2 → **+$160** |
| If B wins | $0 at Book 1, +$160 at Book 2 → **+$160** |
| Locked profit | **≈ $160** (80% conversion at these prices; real prices give less) |

## What it adds up to

- A legal US state typically has several to a dozen licensed books. At
  roughly $100–$300 locked per sign-up offer, **a one-time total of a few
  hundred to ~$1,500 per person** is a realistic range, plus occasional
  smaller ongoing promos ("profit boosts", "no-sweat bets").
- **Risks:** price moves between placing the two legs, voided bets,
  misread terms, the hedge capital tied up for days, and books restricting
  accounts that only bet promos.
- **Requirements:** 21+, physically in a state where sports betting is legal,
  your own accounts, honest information. One account per person per book.
  Never use other people's identities.

## Why it's in the report

It is the only thing in this whole experiment that reliably produces cash
whatever the outcome. And it proves the point the rest of the data made: the
money isn't in out-predicting markets, it's where someone is *paying* you
(here, a marketing budget). It's capped and one-time, not a system that scales.

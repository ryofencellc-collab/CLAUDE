What this proves:
- **The filter works.** Most listings marketed as "cash flow" don't cash flow after real expenses (vacancy, repairs, capital reserves and management take 34% of rent).
- **The sweet spot is duplexes with two 2-bedroom units** priced near $145k or less. 1-bedroom units and 2-bedroom houses don't earn enough.
- **Seller financing helps only at 6% or less.** At 8% it's worse than a bank loan.
- **Volume is the job.** Expect about 1 in 10 listings to pass, and about 1 in 3 offers to be accepted. To buy one property, screen about 30 listings and make 3–5 offers.

Assumptions on every deal: rent set at an estimate of what the housing agency will approve as reasonable for the area, *not* the payment standard; property tax about 2% of price (the Indiana cap for rentals); $15k rehab placeholder; water and sewer paid by the landlord on duplexes.

## 2. Rent ceilings (verify before any offer)

- HUD FY2027 Fair Market Rent, Indianapolis-Carmel (effective Oct 1, 2026): 1BR $1,336, 2BR $1,536, 3BR $1,991, 4BR $2,425.
- Indianapolis Housing Agency (IHA) payment standards on its own site: 2BR $1,619, 3BR $2,110 (undated). The agency sets standards by zip code at 90–110% of HUD's zip-level Fair Market Rents.
- IHA paused processing new rent-increase requests on voucher units into mid-2026. Underwrite with no rent increases for year one.
- Indiana law lets landlords decline vouchers (IC 36-1-3-8.5). Section 8 is a choice we make per unit.

## 3. Team, in hiring order

1. **Investor-friendly agent (Indianapolis MLS).** They send every duplex under $170k in the target zips the day it lists. I screen each one within 24 hours.
2. **Property manager with Section 8 experience.** Lead to vet: HARVEY Property Management Group (Section 8 focus, 3.9 stars on Yelp from 17 reviews). Get two more names from the agent. Avoid managers whose tenant reviews are mostly 1-star.
3. **Real estate attorney (Indiana).** Reviews every seller-financing note and mortgage.
4. **Inspector plus sewer-scope company.** The agent refers these.
5. **Insurance agent.** Landlord (DP-3) policy with loss-of-rent coverage.

### Property manager interview (they must answer every question)
1. How many Housing Choice Voucher units do you manage in Marion County right now?
2. What's your first-try inspection pass rate? How many days does an IHA inspection take to schedule?
3. Average days from move-out to a new voucher tenant moving in?
4. How do you screen: rental history, eviction search, landlord references, home visit?
5. How many evictions did you file last year, as a percentage of units?
6. Fee for management, leasing, renewals and markup on maintenance?
7. Do you send monthly statements and annual 1099s? Can I see a sample?
8. Give me 3 current owner-clients I can call.

## 4. Seller-financing letter (to long-time, free-and-clear owners)

> Hi [Owner name],
>
> I'm a local rental investor interested in buying [address] as-is. I can close quickly with no agent fees to you.
>
> If you'd rather keep a monthly income than take a lump sum, I'll pay you [15%] down and send you monthly payments at [5.5–6%] interest for [10] years. Your attorney records a mortgage on the property, so it secures the debt. Payments spread over years can also spread your capital-gains tax (ask your CPA).
>
> If you'd prefer cash, I can do that too.
>
> [Name] · [phone] · [email]

Who to send it to: county records list of owners who've held for 15+ years with no mortgage and own 1–4 unit properties in the target zips. PropStream or DealMachine pulls this list. Send 300 letters per batch.

## 6. Approval flow

1. I add each screened deal to the Deal Desk.
2. "Numbers pass" unlocks **Approve offer**. You approve, and the agent writes the offer at or below the max price, with inspection and financing contingencies.
3. Once under contract, the inspection, sewer scope, title and rent check clear in the "Before closing" section.
4. Close, the manager places a voucher tenant, and we track real numbers against the paper numbers for 6 months.
